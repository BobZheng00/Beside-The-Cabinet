# Balance (draft, 2026-10-07)

What the app is for, widened. The user, 2026-10-07: LeetCode and Steam are a small part of it. Any learning or good habit can earn play time, and the aim is a good balance of work, life and games. Talked through with the user the same day. **Draft:** sections marked *proposed* wait for the user's word.

## Where it starts from

- **One way to earn:** accepted LeetCode submissions. Easy pays 1 coin (15 minutes of play), Medium 2, Hard 4, and a star coin on a streak. Focus sessions, standing up and plans she checks in on pay nothing.
- **One way to spend:** Steam games (the registry's `Running` flag).
- **Saving:** coins last until Monday 04:00, when the week's unplayed time expires (`WeeklyReset`, "CASH BOX COLLECTED"). Debt carries over.
- **Setup** asks for a LeetCode username and checks that it exists.
- **Already there for balance:** focus sessions with their breaks, the stand-up nudge, check-ins, quiet hours, the agenda.

## Three ways to earn (agreed)

Sorted by how sure the cabinet can be that it happened. The less it knows, the less it pays.
1. **Checked:** LeetCode, as now. Later, other sites with real APIs, as connectors.
2. **Timed:** focus sessions. They cover any work or study, on the PC or off it (a book, a course, coursework).
3. **Told:** the player's own list of activities, each with its worth, and proof when it asks for some.

The LeetCode username at setup becomes optional.

## Focus pays by the minute (decided)

Focus lengths are the player's choice (5 minutes to 2 hours), so focus pays by the minute, not by the part.
- **The rate:** a coin (15 minutes of play) for every hour of focus, 1 to 4. The user asked whether a coin every 25 minutes (3 to 5) was too much; it is. Four hours of real focus, about what a day of deep work holds, then earns an hour of play by itself; LeetCode and activities add to it, up to the daily cap. A setting.
- Minutes count while a focus part runs: not in breaks, not while paused, not while a game is running.
- **Paid at once, however short** (the user, 2026-10-07: "what if the user just focused half an hour?"): when a focus part ends, is skipped or is stopped, its minutes pay straight away. Half an hour adds 7:30 to the time: the screen flashes +7:30, the fuel bar fills, and a coin drops whenever the bar completes a whole 15 minutes. She mentions it as the part ends. The coins themselves don't change (smaller 5-minute coins would be the other way, with new art).

## A daily cap, and unplayed time fades (decided)

**The cap** is how much play can be earned in a game day (04:00 to 04:00), from everything together. It is only on what is earned that day: what is left from before (after the fade) is not part of it (the user, 2026-10-07).
- **Monday to Thursday 1:30, Friday 2:00, Saturday and Sunday 2:30** (the user's numbers), adjustable.
- *Proposed:* a change counts from the next day, so the cap can't be raised in the moment.
- *Proposed, unanswered:* paying back debt doesn't count towards it: earnings still pay debt first, and the cap limits only new play. Otherwise a deep debt could take days of earning and feel hopeless.
- Past the cap no more coins drop that day. The work still counts in the day's record and the streak, and she says so.

**The fade:** unplayed time decays over days, so it can't be saved up for the weekend.
- **Each night at 04:00, half of what wasn't played fades** (the user: "half is ok for now").
- Savings then never pass one day's cap: a day adds at most a cap, and a night halves what there is. So the most play in one day is two days' worth, and only after several days without any. *Stricter, if wanted:* three quarters fade, or all of it.
- It replaces Monday's expiry. Debt never fades. As with the weekly reset, a game running at 04:00 puts the fade off until it ends.
- In the morning the screen shows what faded overnight, and she may mention it.

**The streak** counts the days that earned anything (*proposed*; not in the first build, which keeps the LeetCode streak as it is). The week's numbers stay, for her and the attract screen, split by source.

## Activities you tell her about (agreed; details *proposed*)

**Not set by the player** (the user, 2026-10-07: a player who sets their own proof would choose "never"). Neither what an activity is worth nor how much proof it needs is the player's to set.

**What one is worth:** activities pay like problems: small 1 coin, medium 2, big 4.
- The first time the player tells her about one, she sizes it (by effort and length) and they confirm; it is remembered, so the gym always pays the same. The list builds itself this way.
- The player can see the list and take things off, and ask her to size something again with a reason, but not set prices.
- She decides whether something counts at all: not ordinary life (eating, a shower), and not what focus or LeetCode already pays for.
- A rule of thumb she follows: pay for what the player wants more of but finds hard to start, and leave out what they already enjoy, so it stays fun.
- Each counts at most once a day.

**Logging one:**
- on the CRT, in an app with the list as buttons;
- by telling her: she logs it with a tool (`log_activity`), the amount from its size and never a number of her own, and a tap on her card confirms it, so "I'm going to the gym" isn't taken for "I went";
- at a check-in: something on the agenda can be tied to an activity ("gym at 18:00"), and when she asks how it went and it happened, she logs it.

**Proof** (the user's idea: a photo from outside or a screenshot of the phone's step count, which she judges by how sure she is; fun, not annoying). How often she asks is the cabinet's rule, which she applies:
- by size: small things rarely, medium ones sometimes, big ones usually;
- by track record: proof that convinced her makes her ask less; proof that didn't, more for a while;
- when something doesn't add up: a two-hour walk while the record of days has the player at the PC throughout;
- things that can't be photographed (meditating, calling family) stay small and never need proof.

A photo or a screenshot, handed over as any picture is (below). She looks at it as a friend would first: the view, the weather, the steps, the meal. Then she judges:
  - sure: the slip is stamped and the coins drop;
  - unsure: half;
  - not convinced: nothing yet; a better picture the same day still pays.
- The app tells her what it can check: when a photo was taken, from its metadata when it has some (messaging apps often strip it). A screenshot shows its own date.
- With a model that can't see pictures, proof is skipped and the player's word counts.
- Never a lecture, and never asking twice for the same thing.

**Her manner is her character's** (the user, 2026-10-07: "what if the card says she is conscientious, gentle and considerate?"). The app decides what happens: whether she asks, the verdict, the amount, how often. Her character card decides how she says it: a gentle, considerate character says "I'm not quite sure about this one; could you show me another?" and never teases. Her prompt says what to get across, never the tone; the same holds for bedtime, long play and patterns. Her prompt's "you can encourage, remind and tease" goes when that paragraph is rewritten for the new rules (step 2).
- *To check:* reading steps directly. Phones keep them (Apple Health, Android's Health Connect) with no way in from a PC that we know of, so a screenshot is the practical proof.

### Activities: built (2026-10-08)

Step 4 of the order below.
- **The ledger** (tested): `ActivityDone` pays a share of an activity's worth like a problem of its size (small as Easy, medium as Medium, big as Hard), once a game day: a share paid before tops up (half, then the rest), nothing when it was paid in full; under the day's cap like everything else (`ActivityPaid`; the day's record keeps each activity's share, `DayRecord.Activities`).
- **Her side** (`Activities`, tested): her list of what counts (`activities` in her store: a name, a size, whether it can be photographed), her convictions on the player's proofs (`proofs`) and what waits for proof on a day (`claims`). `log_activity` (inside the app): a name she knows keeps its size (matched whatever the case or a leading "the", "a", "my"); a new one needs hers; the player confirms on her card (Yes, count it / Not yet; no Always, and Auto doesn't skip it); then the cabinet pays, or wants proof. `judge_proof` (inside the app): only for an activity waiting today and with a picture in front of her; sure pays in full and settles it, unsure half (it keeps waiting, so a clearer picture pays the rest), doubtful nothing yet. A photo whose EXIF says another day counts half at most. With a model that can't see pictures no proof is asked for. The tools' results say what to do (ask once, briefly; don't lecture) and never how to sound.
- **The cabinet's rule** (`Activities.Wanted`): never for what can't be photographed; always when it doesn't add up (it took longer than the player has been away from the PC); otherwise by chance: 15% small, 35% medium, 70% big; halved after five convincing proofs or more among the last ten, doubled after a doubtful one (each sure +1, unsure 0, doubtful -2).
- **The cabinet:** her card confirms (`PermissionCard.Confirm`); the screen flashes STAMPED or HALF with the activity and the time it earned, and coins drop in the colour of its size; DONE on the CRT (`DoneApp`): what counts, three to a page, each with its coins and COUNTED, PHOTO? or DID IT (the tap is the player's word: no card), and × then FORGET? to take one off; logged there, she is told and says it in her own words (asking for a photo when the cabinet wants one). Her cabinet block lists what counts, what counted today and what waits for proof.
- **Forgetting the player** (memory book) forgets their activities, proofs and claims too.
- **Not yet:** tying an agenda item to an activity on its own (she can log it at a check-in, with the tool), and coins of a look of their own.

## Handing her things: pictures and files (agreed; how *proposed*)

Proof needs it, and it is useful on its own.
- **Drop or paste.** A file dropped on the slip, or a picture or file pasted into it (Ctrl+V), shows as a chip in the slip and goes with the next message. Dropped on her, a picture with a character card inside stays a card (as now); any other picture is handed to her.
- **Pictures:** she sees them when the model can; otherwise she says she can't. *To check:* image support through Microsoft.Extensions.AI's `DataContent` in both clients (OpenAI-style and Anthropic).
- **Text and code files** go in as text, up to a size. **PDFs** go as documents to providers that read them, otherwise as their text. *To check.*
- **A screenshot when asked** ("look at my screen"): she asks first with the card, as before acting outside the app, since a screenshot can show anything; then she takes the screen or one window. Never on her own.
- **From the phone** (*proposed*): a QR code on the CRT opens a small page the cabinet serves on the home Wi-Fi; the player picks a photo and it lands in the slip. No account and no cloud; only while the code shows, with a one-time key in it. *To check:* Windows asks once to let the app take connections on the private network.
- **Afterwards** she keeps what she saw in words, not the picture: pictures are large and would cost every later request. The file isn't kept.

### Handing her things: built (2026-10-08)

Step 3 of the order below.
- **Her side** (`Attachment`, `Conversation`, tested): what is handed over goes with that one turn: pictures and PDFs as they are (Microsoft.Extensions.AI's `DataContent`; checked against the mock: OpenAI-style `image_url` and `file` parts, Anthropic `image` and `document` blocks), a text file's words as text (60,000 characters at most). The turn asks her to begin with `[saw: …]` for each; that line is kept in the player's line in its place (`[picture: steps.jpg, taken 2026-10-07 18:05: a step counter at 8,214]`), and a text file of 2,000 characters or fewer word for word. A model that turns pictures down (400, 413, 415 or 422 with something in it) is asked again without them and told to say it can't see them; it is remembered (`Conversation.Unseeing`, settings `UnseeingModels`) and sent none again. `Exif` reads a JPEG's orientation and when it was taken; she is told the time ("taken Wednesday 7 October 2026, 18:05"), for proof in step 4. Stage directions may now be 400 characters long, for a `[saw]`.
- **The cabinet:** `Handing` makes pictures ready: turned upright by their EXIF, the long side at most 1568 px, photos as JPEG and anything else as PNG (a busy screenshot over 3.5 MB as JPEG); files up to 20 MB, PDFs 10 MB; HEIC, archives, programs and Office files are refused with a word on the screen. The slip (`ChatBox`) holds up to four as chips above it (a thumbnail or a badge, × to take one back) and sends them with the next message, typed or said; Enter sends them with no words too. Its paper clip offers "Picture or file…" (a file dialog), "Your screen" and "From your phone…". Ctrl+V with no text on the clipboard hands over a picture or files copied in Explorer (`ClipboardFiles`). Dropped on her, a picture without a character card in it, a PDF or a text file is handed over; models, cards and voices are added as before. Without her mind connected the screen says to connect it.
- **Her screen tool** `look_at_screen` (asks first; Always covers every screenshot): the screen the pointer is on (`Screenshot`, GDI), taken when allowed; her reply says a few words, then she is shown it in a turn of its own and answers. A test run never takes the real screen (a stand-in picture, `fakescreen`, or none).
- **From the phone** (`PhoneCard`, `PhonePage`, `QrCode`): the card shows a QR code (our own encoder: byte mode, level L, versions 1 to 5; tested against the standard's worked example and format table, and read back by a decoder written apart) for a page the cabinet serves on the home network (a private IPv4 address with a gateway, a free port) behind a 96-bit key in its path, only while the card shows (ten minutes at most). The page has one button to choose photos (or take one); each is sent as is (`fetch`, the file as the body) and lands on the slip. Anything else is "not here". In a test run it listens on this PC alone, so Windows never asks.
- **To check on the real PC:** the phone page with a real phone (Windows' firewall question the first time; an iPhone's photos arriving as JPEG), Win+Shift+S then Ctrl+V, and a real model looking at a screenshot.

## Rest and sleep (agreed; details *proposed*)

**The mechanism is there.** Every 15 seconds the cabinet checks the conditions for check-ins and standing up (`Stool.MaybeCheckIn`, `MaybeStandUp`). A nudge that is due goes on her queue and she says it in her own words once she's free; in a game or full screen it shows as a ticket. Reminders from the agenda are the separate, scheduled path. New nudges join the same loop.

**A record of days,** on this PC only: when the player was first and last at the PC, what they played and when, how long they focused, what they earned and from what. Kept for a few months; theirs to see and clear. She gets a short summary of it, as she gets the cabinet's state. It gives the day's view on the CRT (focused 3h, the gym and a walk, played 1h) and her sense of patterns.

**Bedtime** (the player's own): she asks once; if they never say, after a week or so she suggests one from when their nights usually end (the record of days), and they confirm it (the user: "either infer or ask"). Until step 5 it is set in the tray.
- 30 minutes before, a word to start winding down;
- at bedtime, time to sleep;
- once more half an hour later, then quiet. In a game, tickets.
- Quiet hours still keep check-ins and standing up away; bedtime nudges come regardless, since they are for exactly then.

**Play after bedtime costs more** (decided: "a good design"). From bedtime until the cabinet's day ends at 04:00, play costs ×1.5; in debt it stacks with debt's ×1.5 (2.25×). Only with a bedtime the player has confirmed, never one guessed. The screen shows LATE NIGHT while it holds, and a ticket says when it starts. A session that runs past 04:00 keeps the late price until it ends (the day turns over when it does). Part of the ledger, so built in step 2.

**Long play:** after two hours in one session she suggests a break: eyes, a stretch, water. In a game, a ticket.

**Patterns she notices,** at most once a day, when the player is back and free, and kindly:
- nights drifting later ("the third night past two");
- long stretches without a break;
- days without play while time is banked: rest counts too.

What the record shows is what is; the bedtime is what the player chose. She never takes a late pattern as the target, though she may suggest getting there in steps.

## Help with learning (agreed; the tools spec's §1, widened)

LeetCode hints become the first case of help with anything being learnt:
- hints in tiers for any subject (a nudge, the approach, an outline; the answer only if asked twice), as a rule in her prompt;
- reading window titles when asked for help, to see what the player is on: a LeetCode tab, a PDF, a course video, their editor;
- LeetCode's problem data as one small built-in source;
- web search and page reading for everything else, built together with it.

## The ledger: built (2026-10-07)

Step 2 of the order below.
- **Core** (`Ledger`, tested): `Focused` (a focus part's time) pays `FocusPerPlay` to one (`FocusPaid`); every earning goes through one payout: debt first, then new play up to the day's room, the rest over the cap (`Payout`: earned, debt paid, credited, over the cap, coins), and `CapReached` once the day is full. A full tank becomes a credit, except the token in the slot while a game runs. The weekly expiry is gone: at each new game day (`TurnDay`, put off while a game runs, until it ends or the next earning) half of the unplayed time fades for each night that passed (`Faded`), today's record joins `Days` (120 kept), and the new day takes the cap and bedtime the rules hold then (so a change counts from the next day). The week's numbers still start again on Monday. Past the day's bedtime (`GameCalendar.IsLateNight`; also after 04:00 while the session that crossed it lasts) every minute costs `LateNightMultiplier`, debt included, announced once a night (`LateNight`). `DayRecord`: the day's cap, bedtime, what faded, earned, over the cap, debt paid, solves, focused, played, the late extra, and when the player was first and last at the PC (input in the last minute) and last played.
- **The cabinet:** a focus part pays as it runs out or is skipped or stopped (`Focus.TimeFocused`, without paused time or time with a game running); the screen flashes "+7:30 / FOCUS 30M" (nothing under two minutes), a coin drops when the fuel bar completes one, TODAY'S CAP when the cap cut it, CAP and NEW DAY, LATE NIGHT (and a ticket in a game); the status line says LATE NIGHT or PAST BEDTIME, and the countdown runs at the late pace; an attract page shows today (earned against the cap, focused, played); the cabinet rises for focus payouts and the fade as for coins. Tray → **Balance…** (`BalanceSettings`): the three caps and the bedtime, saying changes count from tomorrow. Settings: `WeekdayCapMinutes`, `FridayCapMinutes`, `WeekendCapMinutes`, `Bedtime` ("23:30"), `FocusPerPlay` (settings.json only), `SetUp`.
- **First run:** Enter with no LeetCode name goes on without one; the status line then shows the player's initials.
- **Her side:** the cabinet block says what is banked and that half fades each night, focus's rate, today against its cap, what was done today, and the bedtime (and when it is past); her prompt's account of the rules is rewritten (and "tease" is gone); events she witnesses include focus payouts, the cap, the late price and the fade. Canned lines: the cap, late night, the new day (a focus payout has none: her line about the turn says it).
- **Dev:** `focuspaid:30`, `bedtime:2330`, `nextday`, `balance`.
- **Not yet:** the streak still counts LeetCode days only; activities, proof, rest and sleep nudges and patterns are later steps.
