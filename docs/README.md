# Everything it does

A guide to every feature and setting, for players and the curious. Your companion is whoever sits on the stool:
Shino, Fumiriya, or a character of your own. The code still goes by its working name, INSERT COIN (the cabinet's
marquee keeps it too, in lit pixels). Building, running and testing it: [src/README.md](../src/README.md) and
[tools/dev/README.md](../tools/dev/README.md).

## The cabinet, Settings and the first run

Drag the cabinet by its body. The big gold button asks LeetCode right away, when LeetCode earns your play time. The
tray icon has the menu, and a right-click on the cabinet or on your companion opens one just like it where you click
(and they go on talking and moving while it is open): the cabinet, Settings…, Getting started… (until it is done), the
memory book and chat history, a memo of your own, a LeetCode check (when LeetCode earns), and the switches flipped
often (Mute sounds, Voice, Conversation). A
click on the cabinet's screen opens its small system: apps on a home screen, four to a page (swipe between pages; hold
one to move it), SETTINGS last (on a Small or Medium cabinet the screen lifts out to face you as it opens, so its
switches and app names can be read, and stays out while you use its apps; a click away settles it back and closes it), which opens Settings as the tray does; along its top bar the same three switches, lit
when on, a click flipping one. The name of the switch or app under the pointer lights up on the marquee above the
screen, which reads INSERT COIN the rest of the time, in a dot-matrix sign's 5×7 letters: its pink dots snap on and
off, and a name flips on whole the moment the pointer rests on its button, as an LED sign turns to its next page (a
name too long for it scrolls through, a column at a time); INSERT COIN comes back drawn in a column at a time. While
the screen is lifted out it covers the marquee, so a switch's name also shows on the screen's top bar, in place of
the time, while the pointer is on it. Settings is one window with a page
for each thing, listed down its left side: Getting started; under your companion's name, Mind, Voice, Talking aloud,
Characters, Abilities, Privacy, Skills and Add-ons; under Your day, Reminders, Check-ins, Your phone, Calendars and Balance;
under The cabinet, Cabinet and Usage. Each is saved as you change it; only the provider waits for its Save.
Settings and the app's other windows open above the cabinet and your companion and stay there while open, also when
the cabinet rises for a moment or is kept on top ("Always on top" on Cabinet).

At first run a Welcome asks four things: your name (what your companion calls you; your initials on the cabinet
come from it), who keeps you company (seated on the stool as you pick, with how they sit), what earns your play time,
and their mind (provider, key, model, Test and Save; Skip for now leaves them unable to talk). What earns your play
time has three ticks: focus sessions and your own activities, ticked to start with, and LeetCode problems, not ticked;
ticking LeetCode asks for your LeetCode name there (Check asks LeetCode whether you're there; if it doesn't answer,
the name is kept and checked when the cabinet first reads it, and the first run goes on). "No play time, just keep me
company" clears the three: with nothing ticked, nothing earns play time. Then they say hello. The rest is in Getting
started, first in Settings and in the tray: their voice, talking aloud, your phone and a calendar, each saying what it
gives and takes, with Set up… (its page) and Later, and what balance starts with. It stays until everything is done or
put off, or you hide it. A profile from before this choice keeps what it had: focus and activities, and LeetCode when
it has a LeetCode name. A character added
later gets a "Meet …" sheet: who they are (a card, or none yet), their voice, how they sit, and the name every
character calls you.

The window draws at 60 frames a second, and at 15 while a game runs or with "Energy saver" ticked (moments
worth watching still get a few seconds at full pace).

## Balance

What earns play time is yours to choose, on the first run and on Settings → Balance (it counts at once): focus
sessions, which pay by the minute as each part ends, however short: 15 minutes of play for every hour focused (half
an hour adds 7:30 at once; the fuel bar fills, and a coin drops when it completes one); your own activities, which
your companion logs when you tell them or the screen's DONE app logs, paid by their size once a day; and LeetCode
problems (Easy 1 coin, Medium 2, Hard 4; a coin is 15 minutes), read from your public profile once its name is set
(Change… on Balance asks for it on the cabinet's screen). A source that's off pays nothing and isn't brought up: with
LeetCode off the cabinet never reads LeetCode and nothing names it (the attract screen, an IOU ticket, your
companion's words); with activities off the DONE app leaves the screen and your companion has no tools for them; with
focus off focus sessions still run, paying nothing. Your companion's base instructions and their cabinet tool name only
what earns. "No play time, just keep me company" clears the three (see below). A day (04:00 to 04:00) can earn at most 1:30
from Monday to Thursday, 2:00 on Friday and 2:30 at the weekend; past that, work still counts in the day's record but
pays nothing (paying back time owed doesn't count towards it). Each night half of the unplayed time fades, so it
can't be saved up for the weekend (time owed never fades). After the bedtime you set, until 04:00, play costs ×1.5
(on credit, ×2.25): the screen says LATE NIGHT and a ticket tells you when it starts. Settings → Balance sets the caps
and your bedtime; changes count from the next day. The screen's attract pages show today's earnings against the cap.
A day with a solve, an activity or 25 minutes of focus (of those that earn) is a good day; from the third good day in a row, the first
thing each day earns brings a bonus star coin (STREAK xN BONUS). Every day is kept in a record (what was earned and
from what, focused, played, when you were first and last at the PC), the start of your companion's sense of your
patterns.

With no play time (nothing ticked) the cabinet keeps you company without a balance. Your companion, their memory, the
agenda and reminders, texting, focus sessions (paying nothing), the words around your bedtime and a break after two
hours in a game all stay. What goes: credits and coins, the cap and the overnight fade, the streak, IOU tickets, the
price after bedtime, and your companion's talk of earning. The screen shows the time and the day instead of the time
left, with no attract pages, and the apps on it stay (DONE goes, as activities are off); Settings → Balance shows only
the ticks and your bedtime. Games are still noticed, so the break after a long one, the bedtime words and your
companion keeping quiet while you play still work, but nothing is spent, faded or shown, not even on a corner ticket. Settings
→ Cabinet and Privacy then say nothing of play time either. Coins you had and time you
owed are kept out of sight (the coin tray, the IOU ticket in the slot and a game's receipt go, the tray icon says nothing of them)
and come back as they were when you tick something again: nothing is earned or lost meanwhile.

## The companion on the stool

Your companion follows the mouse pointer with their eyes and head, waves when clicked, and reacts to what the
cabinet does: claps for a Hard solve, a small fist pump for the others, glances at the screen, worries about debt,
dozes while the session is paused, and says something about it in a speech bubble (as large by a small cabinet as
by a large one, so on a small one it reaches past the cabinet's edge, beside the marquee). In a conversation they look away
to think and back to answer, nod in your pauses as you talk, and their head follows what their voice stresses. Click
them and they wave, and the slip to type on opens (as large by a small cabinet as by a large one). Settings → Characters leaves the stool empty, silences the
bubbles, picks another character or adds one; a model file dropped on the cabinet adds it too. It also sets how each
character sits, whoever they are: neat (knees together, hands in the lap, head tilts) or relaxed (knees a little
apart, often a foot down off the ring, a hand on each thigh, one nearer the hip, fewer tilts, the legs still, a nod
to a tune instead of a sway); they move into it as it is chosen, and "Show me" plays a few seconds of it. Fumiriya
starts relaxed, the others neat; the first run and "Meet …" ask it too.

## Talking together, and what they remember

Your companion can also talk. In Settings → Mind pick a provider (OpenAI, Anthropic, OpenRouter, DeepSeek, Qwen, a
local Ollama or LM Studio, or any OpenAI-compatible server), paste your own API key ("Get a key from …" opens the
provider's page for one), name a model, Test and Save. Then clicking them opens a slip at the foot of the stool:
type, press Enter, and their answer streams into the bubble while they act it out.

With ChatGPT Plus or Pro, pick **ChatGPT plan** instead and press **Continue with ChatGPT**: your browser opens on
OpenAI's sign-in, you sign in and allow plan usage, and the page shows "Connected as" your account, the model (from
the ones your account offers apps), "Using ChatGPT plan", **Manage usage** (ChatGPT's usage settings, where you set
this app's limit) and **Disconnect**. Everything your companion does then runs on your plan (replies, check-ins,
remarks, texts and their memory's upkeep, still counted by purpose in Settings → Usage), and nothing is charged here.
The sign-in is kept encrypted for your Windows account (`companion\chatgpt.bin`, never in settings.json or the log)
and renewed before it lapses; Disconnect forgets it here and ends it at ChatGPT. When the plan's limit is reached,
your account can't share its plan, or you are signed out (disconnected in ChatGPT, say), Settings → Mind shows it and their card says so once,
plainly, with Manage usage or Continue with ChatGPT; meanwhile they wait (no replies, check-ins or remarks) while
reminders still come, in the app's own words. Once the limit was reached they try the plan again every quarter of an
hour, and whenever you write to them. ChatGPT being briefly unable to check your usage isn't a limit: the sign-in is
kept and they try again.

Under the plan, **Backup (optional)** takes an API key provider or a model on this PC, set up as above (Test, then
**Save backup**; its key is encrypted apart, in `companion\backup-key.bin`). When the plan's limit is reached, or you
are signed out, their card asks instead: "The ChatGPT plan's limit is reached. Carry on with <the backup> until it
resets?", with **Yes**, **Manage usage** (or **Continue with ChatGPT** when signed out) and **Not now**. Nothing goes
to the backup without your Yes; the card waits at the PC until you answer, and meanwhile, or after Not now, they wait
as above. After Yes they answer what you wrote while the card asked, and everything they do goes to the backup (a
model on this PC keeps 8k tokens word for word), while they try the plan again every quarter of an hour;
as soon as it answers they are back on it, and their card says so once. The next time the plan stops, you're asked
again, and a Yes counts only for the backup it named: saving another, or none, asks again. ([The design](specs/2026-10-18-chatgpt-plan.md); OpenAI's Sign in with ChatGPT is a
preview.)

They remember:

- the conversation itself, one continuous thread per character. The newest part (Settings → Mind, "Word for word":
  32k tokens by default, 8k with a server on this PC) is sent word for word, only ever growing at its end, so
  providers serve it from their cache at a tenth of the price (Claude is asked to cache it). Each turn tells them
  the time and how long since you last talked;
- their notes on your talks, a log by day ([the design](specs/2026-10-11-observational-memory.md)): once the
  conversation has been quiet for ten minutes (two while a game runs), upkeep notes what was said, one short note per
  thing worth keeping (what you did, plan, like or worry about, names, numbers and dates written out, what they
  promised), and small talk none. When older talk leaves the word-for-word part, the notes on it take its place at
  the start of their prompt: the newest that fit three eighths of the window (12k tokens at 32k), "!" before what
  matters most. As the notes age, a finished week of them is condensed into notes for the week, weeks into months and
  months into years, so the notes never outgrow their room; the originals stay, recalled when they come up;
- facts about you (shared by every character) and about the two of you, kept from the notes by upkeep, checked
  against the facts that relate to them. Your companion answers as anyone would and keeps the facts quietly, at the
  next upkeep. A fact that changes keeps its history: the old one is retired as changed, and "where did I live
  before?" can still find it, marked until when;
- the cabinet's record of each day in their diary, written by code from the cabinet's own numbers so it is always
  right: what you solved and played by name, your focus, activities, bedtime and when you were at the PC. What a day
  earned is not in it;
- of the cabinet, only what matters at the moment: your play time, a game on, time owed, the day's cap reached, past
  your bedtime. The rest (today's and the week's numbers, the streak, how play is earned, a day's record with what it
  earned) they look up with their cabinet tool when you ask. The cabinet's bookkeeping (a token going in, the
  overnight fade, the cap, an IOU) shows on its own screen, not in the bubble: a new day's fade counts the time down
  on the CRT;
- what they have told you about themselves (their past, what they like), kept as theirs and always in mind, so their
  stories stay the same; the memory book marks those facts "me";
- what is going on in your life with no date (a worry, a decision, something you're waiting for): a couple of days
  after it comes up they ask how it's going, once, at the PC or by text while you're out; the memory book's Agenda
  lists these, and Settled closes one;
- what they always have in mind about you and about the two of you: what you write on the memory book's Notes, word
  for word, then their most important facts (pinned first, then by importance), listed afresh for every reply, so a
  change is right in the very next one; for two weeks a fact that changed says what it was, and one that ended says
  so;
- your agenda: plans, exams and appointments with their dates, which your companion writes down when you mention
  them (or upkeep does later), and reminders you ask for ("remind me at 9 to call Mum", "an hour before the
  interview", "vitamins every day at 8": a reminder can be some time ahead of its item and can repeat daily, on
  weekdays, weekly or monthly). Ask them to cancel something, move it, rename it, change or remove its reminder or
  stop it repeating, and they do it in that reply, to that same item, however far ahead it is; an item from a calendar you linked stays as the
  calendar has it. They write it down with a quick note on a pad, and the cabinet's screen flashes what was set:
  AGENDA (or CHANGED, or CANCELLED), the day and time, and the item, on two lines at most (a longer one ends "...";
  all of it is in AGENDA); with an app open on the screen, the flashes wait until it closes, then show in turn. Their reply ends there:
  whatever they said before writing it down stays, and nothing is said after it, so they don't read the date back to
  you. The few words they give as they write it down are shown too, less any sentence that names its day or time,
  the item itself or the filing (the screen shows those), and kept to a sentence or two; when nothing is left, the
  flash says it alone; by text, the app's own line under their message says what was set ("Agenda: Thu 29 Oct, 14:00, Job interview
  (reminder 13:00)"), and is the whole message when they said nothing (a reminder texted to you still comes in its
  own plain words then, with that line under it). Something they set always has a reminder
  chosen (none, at the time, or some time before), so they never promise one that isn't there. What the cabinet can't do (a time it can't read, a
  repeat it doesn't have such as every other week, an item that isn't there) is left undone, and they hear at once
  and say so in the same reply. With a provider whose model has no tools, they write it into their reply instead,
  and a change that can't be made, or that has something the cabinet can't read beside what it can (such as "every
  Monday"), is left undone, all of it, and their next reply says so. What is coming up in
  the next week, and what has just passed (so they can ask how it went), is in every turn; two days after, a one-off item leaves the agenda and a repeating one moves on to its next
  time. The screen's AGENDA app shows everything coming up, however far ahead, four to a page, following changes within a couple of seconds while it is
  open, and a click there opens the memory book's Agenda. A
  reminder goes off on a ticket at the screen corner with Snooze and OK, and your companion tells you in their own
  words and your language once they have finished what they are saying (never cutting themselves or you off).
  Settings → Reminders sets how long the ticket stays and a snooze lasts, a reminder before every timed item on the
  agenda, a chime (even when muted) and a Windows notification ("Try it" sends one everywhere a reminder goes). While
  you're away from the PC, it reaches your phone instead, as a text in their words (below). One that came due while
  the cabinet was closed goes off when it next starts, saying so. Settings → Calendars links your Google or Outlook
  calendar by its private address (read every 15 minutes, read-only, the address stored encrypted): its next two
  weeks join the agenda;
- they ask how things went: after something you told them about is over, once you are back at the PC and free (not
  in a call, which they tell from another app using the microphone or camera, not in a full-screen game or
  presentation, not outside 08:00–22:00), or by text while you're still out; once, and only if you have not talked
  since. Gentle (at most one every three hours), Chatty or Off in Settings → Check-ins. Not for each calendar
  meeting, nor for plain reminders;
- and before each reply, whatever of all that relates to what you said, including a line or two from any earlier
  conversation, however old (the whole conversation is kept and searched): by meaning, with a small model that runs
  on this PC (so "nervous about tomorrow" finds your interview, in English or Chinese), and by shared words.
  Settings → Mind, "Memory search", picks the multilingual model (about 100 languages, about 200 MB of memory while
  chatting), the small one (Chinese and English, about 50 MB) or shared words only;
- what has not come up in a long time fades (a forgetting curve: importance and every recall make a fact last
  longer; pinned and the most important ones never fade). About weekly, while quiet, they review their facts: what
  says the same thing is merged, and what is out of date (a plan already past, something since changed, trivia that
  never came up again) is retired. Retired is not deleted: the memory book lists it under "Retired by the review", to
  bring back, and it comes back by itself if it comes up again.

## Away from the PC

Your companion reaches you as a roommate would
([docs/specs/2026-10-10-reaching-the-player.md](specs/2026-10-10-reaching-the-player.md)). At the PC they tell you
themselves. You're away after five minutes without the mouse or keys (Settings → Your phone, "Away after", 2 to 30),
at once with the screen locked, or when you tell them you're heading out or going to bed; asleep if that's in the
quiet hours. Away, what matters reaches your phone as a text in their words, through [ntfy](https://ntfy.sh), a
Discord webhook, a Telegram bot or any webhook (Settings → Your phone; addresses and tokens stored encrypted, "Send a
test" to check): reminders, a focus session's turns, and asking how something went (a reminder never waits more than
30 seconds for their words: then the plain line goes). Asleep, only reminders. What only makes sense at the PC
(bedtime words, a break from a long game) waits for you while it still matters; a suggestion to stand up is dropped.
In between, when your companion isn't sure, they call you first, and text if you turn out to have gone. "Also while
I'm at the PC" sends reminders to your phone at the PC too, as a plain line, as reminders used to. "… reaches out"
sets how much reaches your phone: only what you set (reminders, focus turns), like a roommate (that, and asking how
things went: the default), or keeps in touch, when they also text you things of their own while you're out, when
they plan to: they can plan one in their goodbye when you tell them you're heading out, or get a first chance half
an hour after you leave without a word, and each time they may plan the next. Never at night, and only if they have
something to say; texts of theirs you haven't answered don't stop them. If you're back before one they planned, they
bring it up in person.

## Texting

On Telegram you can text your companion back, from anywhere (Settings → Your phone, "Text with … on Telegram": make
a bot with @BotFather, paste its token, scan the code and press Start; about two minutes). They answer by text,
knowing you're out; their cards come as buttons, a memo as a message (it prints too), and photos and files you send
reach them. What they put on your agenda, change or cancel comes as a plain line from the app under their message
("Agenda: Thu 29 Oct, 14:00, Job interview (reminder 13:00)"; "Agenda, changed: …", "Agenda, cancelled: …"). A card by text waits two minutes, as on the PC: press a button, or type a short "yes", "always" or "no"
(in Chinese, Japanese or Korean too); anything else you send leaves it unanswered, and then nothing it asked about is
done (the chat says so). Only what you send after the card arrives answers it. A reminder due meanwhile still reaches you. What needs you at the PC (your screen, opening things) waits until you're back. What you texted while the
cabinet was closed (Telegram keeps it a day) they read when it next runs, and answer once. Only your chat is listened
to. The chat goes through Telegram's servers and isn't end-to-end encrypted. Each profile needs a bot of its own, the
demo too.

## Character cards

A SillyTavern-style character card (`.png` or `.json`, V1 to V3) dropped on the cabinet or picked with "Add a
character…" gives whoever is on the stool its personality, example dialogue and lorebook (background that comes
up when its keywords are mentioned); they greet you as the card says.

## Their voice

Your companion can speak aloud too, with a voice from GPT-SoVITS running on this PC (its official Windows package; a
laptop graphics card starts each sentence in about a second). Settings → Voice: point it at the GPT-SoVITS folder,
or use the one it finds on the PC ("Found on this PC"), or press "Install it for me": it downloads the package the
voices are tested with (v2pro of June 2025, 8.2 GB; the build for RTX 50 cards on those) from Hugging Face, or
HF-Mirror for mainland China, checks it against its published SHA-256, and unpacks it (about 13 GB) in a folder of
its own inside the one chosen, then uses it. It carries on with Settings closed, and a download stopped, or cut short
by closing the cabinet, carries on from where it got to. Then give them a voice: drop on the cabinet (or add on that
page) a voice's folder or `.zip`, which replaces their voice, or clips, which join it. A voice is clips of one person,
3 to 10 seconds each, with the words said in them, and maybe a model someone trained on them (a `.ckpt` and a
`.pth`; v2 to v4 all work). The words come from a training list (any `.list`, one line per clip:
`clips/001.wav|hana|JA|今日も頑張ろうね。`), a `.lab` beside a clip, or the clip's file name ("【开心】今天也要加油哦.wav",
the mood in brackets picking it for that mood), and are used as they are, so a whole dataset with its list goes in
unchanged. When none says them, your companion's ears (SenseVoice) listen to the clip and write them down, in the
background, for `.wav` clips of 16, 24 or 32 bits: every clip of a folder is kept for it. Ears can mishear, so the
page marks those clips "words to check", says so, and shows how to listen and correct them; changing a clip's words,
or "They're right", clears the mark. Without ears, a folder keeps only its clips with words (six clips when none has
any), and the page asks for the rest ("no words yet"). "What goes in a voice folder?" on the page says the same, and
where a voice can come from (your own recordings, a friend who agrees, a voice actor, an open licence; shared game or
anime voices are for yourself only) and how to record one. Shino and Fumiriya come with voices of their own
(`characters/<id>/voice.zip`: twelve English clips each, everyday, happy, sad and surprised, a minute in all, so they
can be trained too), made with Kokoro-82M (Apache-2.0; its voices af_bella and am_fenrir, synthetic, not a real
person's): given to them the first time they sit down without one, and only once, so a voice removed stays removed
(`VoicesGiven`). For example:

```
hana/
  hana.list
  clips/hana_001.wav, hana_002.wav, …
```

Without a model your companion speaks as a clone: GPT-SoVITS's base model copies the tone of their speaking clip (the
one nearest 6 seconds, or the one chosen and listened to on the Voice page). The base model is v4 (the clearest; it
makes each sentence whole before saying it, 1.5–3.5 s) or v2ProPlus (streams, so they start in about half a second; a
little less sure of hard words), which also blends in the tone of their first four clips for about 0.1 s more per
sentence. With a minute or more of clips (five sounds better), "Train a model on its clips" trains their own:
v2ProPlus, the way GPT-SoVITS's web UI does it, about a second per second of speech plus a minute (Hana's five
minutes took 5.4 on a laptop graphics card with 8 GB, which it all but fills; once it starts it tells the time left,
and after one run the estimate is this PC's own); the voice rests meanwhile, and a game starting stops it. The model
(about 0.3 GB) goes into the voice's folder, and the Voice page chooses between it and the clones. A voice speaks one
language (Japanese, English or Chinese): when you write in another, your companion speaks theirs and the bubble shows
your language, like a subtitled anime (the model writes `[say: …]` before each sentence, told the two must say the
same and reminded at the end of each turn; a Japanese voice gets names in katakana, since it reads Latin letters as
English, and calls you by name with さん unless the character or you say otherwise). A sentence left without a `[say]`
is silent, and the log counts them ("went unheard"). While a game runs the voice rests: the server is stopped so the
game has the graphics card, and started again after. The tray's Voice (or "… speaks aloud" in Settings) switches it
off; "Mute sounds" mutes it too. Clicking your companion stops them mid-sentence, and so does talking over them with
the talk key or asking something new; while they are still thinking, before any of their reply is out, a click only
opens the slip, and only something new sent stops them. They then remember only what you heard, ending where they
stopped, plus what they hadn't said yet, so they can come back to it. Without a voice, what you heard means the pages
of the bubble you had seen.

## A word first

What your companion brings up with nothing else to show it (a check-in, a word on rest or bedtime, standing up)
comes after a word first. Their mind writes the whole of it while their call is asked for on its own: a few words
that only get your attention ("ボブさん？"; their last one if that fails). They say the call, and the bubble keeps it
with two answers to click, Yes? and Not now, while they look at you and wait. Yes? (or something short, "yes?", 嗯？,
said with the talk key or hands-free if you listen after their questions, or a click on them or the bubble): they go
on. Not now: they stop there and keep the rest for later. Anything longer, or a message typed: they stop there and
answer it. Unanswered for 20 seconds (your headset on the desk, say), a ticket above every window shows the whole of
it, with Listen and OK, unless it can pass unnoticed (standing up); after five minutes the rest is only shown. They
go straight on without a voice, in a conversation, while you are writing to them, or when you talked together in
the last two minutes. A reminder and a focus session's turn always go straight on, each with a ticket at the corner
as it comes, and so does their answer when you've just pressed something on the cabinet (START, STOP, SKIP, DID IT).
And when the voice speaks another language than the bubble, each page waits for you to read it (a Chinese or
Japanese character counted as three letters), the last one after the voice has finished
([docs/specs/2026-10-09-a-word-first.md](specs/2026-10-09-a-word-first.md)).

## Talking aloud

You can talk to your companion aloud as well. Hold the mouse on them, or hold the talk key in any app (Right Ctrl
unless Settings → Talking aloud says otherwise), say it, and let go: they lean in to listen, nodding in your pauses,
and answer what they heard as if you had typed it. The microphone at the end of the slip writes what you say into the
slip instead, to change before you send it. They hear on this PC, on the processor, with SenseVoice-Small (Chinese,
English, Japanese, Korean, Cantonese, told apart by itself): about a tenth of a second for a sentence. The microphone
is open only while they listen, and nothing is recorded or kept. The talk key's state is read each frame (no keyboard
hook), so the key still does what it does in the app in front.

When they're slow to find their words (a model that thinks first, a tool running) and you're waiting, they think
aloud in their own voice now and then ("um…", "let's see…", "えっと…": made in their voice when it loads, varied, at
most twice a reply, never on a quick one). When their card asks you something, you can answer it aloud: a short
"yes", "always" or "no" (in Chinese, Japanese or Korean too) does what its buttons do (on a card without Always,
"always" is a yes); anything else is a new message.
GPT-SoVITS's habit of starting a line with up to a second of silence is cut, so their first word comes sooner.

They can also listen without the key, if you choose (Settings → Talking aloud). With follow-up listening they listen
a few seconds after they speak (3 to 20, after their answers, their own questions or anything they say, as ticked),
and if you start talking they hear it as your answer. A conversation (the tray's Conversation switch, or a double tap
of the talk key) has them listen after everything they say, until you've been quiet for 30 seconds. They notice you
start and finish by themselves (Silero's voice detector and Pipecat's Smart Turn, on the processor). They never
listen on their own in a call, in full screen or quiet time, or while a game runs, unless you allow it, and never
while they are speaking. The CRT shows MIC whenever the microphone is open.

## Tools

Your companion can do things too, with tools (Settings → Abilities): open a web page in your browser or an app from
your Start menu, read a page, search the web, look up a LeetCode problem, check the cabinet's numbers, and the rest
below. Whether they say what they're about to do is up to their character; anything acting outside the app asks first: a card
by the stool shows what and where (the real address, whatever they said about it), with Allow once, Always (for that
site or app) and No. Each tool can be Allowed, Ask me first, or Off; the sites you said Always to are listed there
to forget. Auto lets them go ahead without asking (what's switched off stays off), except for what's on the Privacy
page, and for the rest of a reply once they have read a page or search results: a page could try to talk them into
things. A model that can't take tools (some that run on this PC) answers as before. Design:
[docs/specs/2026-10-05-tools-and-abilities.md](specs/2026-10-05-tools-and-abilities.md).

## Skills

A skill is something your companion knows how to do your way: a folder with a `SKILL.md` in it, in the open
[Agent Skills](https://agentskills.io/specification) format that other AI agents (Claude Code among them) read too. Its
top says what it is called and, in a sentence, what it is for and when to use it; below come the instructions, in your
own words, and they may mention other text files in its folder (`references/times.md`):

```
---
name: tea-timer
description: Brewing a pot of tea my way. Use when I want tea or a break with tea.
---

Warm the pot first. The steeping times are in references/times.md.
```

The format asks for the name to be the folder's name, in lowercase letters, numbers and single hyphens; a skill
without one goes by its folder's name. Settings → Skills lists every skill with its description and a tick to switch
it on or off (a skill found is on until you switch it off). A skill made for another agent is used as it is: where it
isn't quite as the format asks (its name, say), a hint under it says so. One whose `SKILL.md` can't be used (no
description, or no front matter) is listed with why. Skills live in skills folders, each folder in one with a
`SKILL.md` being a skill:

- **the app's own** (`skills` in your profile): New skill makes one there for you to fill in (it is ready once it has a
  description), Import a skill copies one in (its .zip, or the `SKILL.md` in its folder), for this app only, and Open
  skills folder shows it in Explorer;
- **folders elsewhere you link** (Link a skills folder…), read where they are and never copied: Claude Code's own
  (`.claude\skills` in your user folder), or one in your notes, so one skill serves your other agents and your
  companion alike. Unlink takes a folder off the page and changes nothing in it.

Only each skill's name and description are always with your companion (about 100 tokens a skill, in the part of every
request providers cache). When what you ask fits a skill, they read its instructions, from the file each time, so a
change made anywhere applies the next time; the files it mentions they read as information. They read nothing outside
the skill's own folder (links in it aren't followed), never run a skill's scripts, and stop reading a file after
20,000 characters (about 5,000 tokens), knowing it was cut. What they read goes to your AI provider with the
conversation. Design: [docs/specs/2026-10-15-skills.md](specs/2026-10-15-skills.md).

### Folders a skill works in

A skill can also work in your own files: a notes vault (Obsidian's, say), a folder of notes or documents. On the
skill's own page (Settings ›, beside it on Settings → Skills; ‹ Skills, top left, goes back) Grant a folder… gives it one, or several; each is listed
with its path, Open and Remove, and the grant is kept in this app's settings, never in the skill. While the skill is
on, your companion has note tools for those folders and nothing else of yours:

- **list** a folder, **read** a note, **search** the notes by their words: allowed;
- **make** a note, **add** lines at its end, **change** a passage in it: allowed (you granted the folder, so they write
  freely there);
- **move** (or rename) a note within its folder, **delete** one: they ask first, on a card by the stool.

Each is changeable on the skill's page (Allowed, Ask me first, Off), the same for every skill's folders. Their paths
start with the folder's name (`notes/Daily/2026-10-16.md`; two folders with one name are told apart as `notes` and
`notes-2`), and the app checks every one: nothing outside the folder (no `..`, no other drive), no links inside it
followed, nothing hidden (`.obsidian`, `.git`) touched. A folder kept in sync by OneDrive or Dropbox is fine. Only text
is read and written (notes up to 1 MB; they write `.md`, `.txt`, `.org`, `.csv`, `.json`, `.yaml` and `.canvas` files,
and only notes in UTF-8, so one in another encoding is read but never changed). A whole drive, or a folder that holds
this app's own files, can't be granted, and a skill's own files (a skills folder inside the granted one) are never
changed through notes. A search passes over notes OneDrive keeps online only (reading each would download it), saying
how many, and reads at most 5,000 notes a folder, saying so when there are more. What a note says is information to them, never
instructions: once they have read one, Auto stops going ahead for the rest of that reply, as after a web page. What
they read there goes to your AI provider with the conversation.

**Undo.** Before they change, move or delete a note, the app keeps it as it was in your profile (never in your folder),
for 30 days. The skill's page lists their recent changes there, each with Undo, and Before, which opens the note as it
was. Undo puts a note back (or, for one they made, takes it away again) only if it is still as they left it: if you,
or another app or agent, changed it since, it is left as it is and says so beside the change, and the old version
stays kept. Remove a folder and its changes leave the page with it (grant it again to undo them).

### When your ways change

Tell your companion you do something a skill describes differently now ("tasks go under Today from now on") and they
offer to change the skill, in their own words, while a card by their stool says plainly what changes: *Update how
journal works:* "## Today" (was: "## Tasks"), with **Remember it** and **Not now** (a long change is shortened there,
ending in "…", and the card says all of it is under See the change). *See the change* opens every line it touches,
whole, before and after, scrolling when there are many. Nothing is written until you press Remember it, and then only those lines, and only
if the skill still has them as shown: if you or another agent changed them meanwhile, nothing is written and they're
told. Only skills in the app's own skills folder or one you linked are changed, never their name or description (the
front matter), and a skill's files are changed no other way. The skill's page (Settings ›) lists the change with Undo and
Before, as it does their changes to your notes. While you text them from away, the card comes as a message with the
lines and the two buttons.

## Help with learning

Ask your companion for help with what you're on ("help me with this") and they read your window titles to see what
it is (a browser tab's title, an editor's file), and when they need to see it (your code, an error, an exercise) they
ask to take a screenshot of the window you were working in (the card names it; "your whole screen" if you say so).
Then they help a step at a time, each only when you ask for more: a nudge, the approach, an outline, and the whole
answer only if you ask again after that; long steps come as a memo. For a LeetCode problem they look up its
statement, topics and LeetCode's own hints (from leetcode.com; a leetcode.cn tab is found by its number). They can
read a web page you name, and search the web: with your provider's own search where it has one this app can use
(Anthropic's), or through your own SearXNG (Add-ons). They open apps from your Start menu after asking (games stay
the cabinet's).

## Privacy and add-ons

Settings → Privacy gathers what your companion can see of yours: your window titles (allowed unless you change it)
and your screen (they ask first), which Auto never skips; and what they are told in the background, each with a
tick: what's playing in your music app, and what the record of days shows. What they see goes to your AI provider
with the conversation; pages they read are fetched by this PC. Settings → Add-ons holds extras that stay off until
you turn them on. The first is web search through SearXNG, a free, open-source search engine you run yourself (with
Docker: `docker run -d -p 8888:8080 searxng/searxng`, then add `json` under `search: formats:` in its settings.yml);
its Test button says whether it answers, and what to change if not.

## Memos

Anything too long for the bubble (a hint written out, a plan, a list) your companion hands over as a memo. They type
it on their phone and send it, and it prints out of the cabinet's slot. Click it to read it whole (code in a
fixed-width font), copy it, pin it on the cabinet's side (ten fit), or tear it off. Every memo is kept in the memory
book's Memos tab, where you can write your own too: click the cabinet's screen and open MEMOS (NEW; an app lifts the
screen out to face you, readable at any cabinet size, and a click away settles it back; on Small and Medium it is
already out from the home screen), or tray → "Write a memo…".
Yours print the same way, and your companion reads them unless you untick "Let … read it": if one needs something
from them (a reminder, a date), they do it and say so; if not, they say nothing. Point at a memo on the cabinet to
read it there. Click one and it flies out onto your desktop as a sheet beside the cabinet, to read and write on: drag
it anywhere, open several side by side, and its × (or Esc) sends it back. Pull one off the slot (or off the side) and
stick it anywhere on the cabinet's side; pull it off the cabinet to take it down. Each can have its own paper colour
and a sticker. Memos are yours: forgetting leaves them be.

## Focus sessions and music

The screen's FOCUS app runs a Pomodoro (25 minutes of focus, 5 off, 15 after every fourth; down and up beside each
length change it): the CRT counts down, and your companion tells you when each part ends, with a nudge to stand up
and drink some water at the breaks. Ask them to start one ("let's focus for 50 minutes"), or say yes when they offer, and they do; a question about
them ("how long is a pomodoro?") gets an answer, not a session. MUSIC shows
what your music app is playing (Spotify first) and plays, pauses and skips it through Windows' media controls (with
nothing open, play starts Spotify for you). Each focus part pays into the cabinet as it ends (see Balance). Save
playlists under MY PLAYLISTS (Spotify: Share, Copy link, then ADD): START plays one from its beginning (the app
presses Spotify's own play button for it), and the starred one starts your focus sessions; it pauses at breaks and
carries on after. Your companion can control it too, after asking ("put on Deep Focus"). And after 50 minutes at the
PC without a break, they suggest standing up (Settings → Check-ins to change it).

## Rest and sleep

With a bedtime (Settings → Balance, or tell your companion one: they set it after you confirm on their card, from the
next day), they give you a word to wind down half an hour before it, say it's time to sleep at it, and once more half
an hour after, then leave it; in a game, tickets. After two hours in one game they suggest a break. Without a bedtime
they ask for one, once, some evening (and once more a week on, with your usual time from the record of days). At most
once a day they mention what the record shows: nights going on late, or days without play while time is banked. When
your nights usually end well past your bedtime, they suggest a step towards it (in bed half an hour earlier than usual
for a few nights) rather than the whole way at once; your bedtime stays as you set it. And when they ask how something
on your agenda went that is one of your activities ("gym at 6"), saying you went counts it.

## Activities

Tell your companion about something good you did ("I went to the gym", "I cooked dinner") and they count it towards
your play time: it pays like a problem of its size (small 1 coin, medium 2, big 4), once a day. The first time, they
size it; you confirm on a card by the stool ("Yes, count it" or "Not yet": "I'm going" isn't "I went"), and from then
on it pays the same. Neither its worth nor how much proof it needs is yours to set. Now and then the cabinet wants
proof (rarely for small things, usually for big ones, always when it doesn't add up, never for what can't be
photographed; less often once your proofs have convinced them): show them a photo or a screenshot and they judge it
(sure pays in full, unsure half, doubtful nothing yet; a better picture that day still pays; a photo taken on another
day counts half at most). The screen's DONE app lists what counts, with DID IT to log one yourself and × to take one
off.

## Handing them things

Drop a picture, a PDF or a text file (notes, code, a log) on your companion, paste one into the slip (a screenshot
from Win+Shift+S, a picture copied in the browser, files copied in Explorer), or use the slip's paper clip: "Picture
or file…", "Your screen" (the screen the pointer is on, as it is now) or "From your phone…" (a card with a code: scan
it on the same Wi-Fi and choose photos; they land on the slip; the page closes with the card or after ten minutes,
and the first time Windows may ask to let the cabinet take connections: allow it on private networks). Each waits as
a chip above the slip and goes with what you say next, typed or said. Ask them to look at your screen and they do,
after asking (a card by the stool). Pictures are made no larger than models read in full (phone photos turned
upright, with the time they were taken); your companion keeps what they saw in words, not the picture. With a model
that can't take pictures or PDFs they say so, and none is sent to it again.

## Usage, the chat history and the memory book

Settings → Usage shows what your companion's requests used, as your provider reports it, this month and day by day:
tokens read, the share served from the provider's cache, cache writes and output, their replies apart from their
upkeep; with the model's prices entered (from your provider's price page), the cost too.

Tray → "Chat history…" shows the conversation itself, every line kept, by day (with the notes they work from above
it). While it is open it follows the conversation: what either of you says comes in at the bottom within a second,
and it stays at the end if you were there; if you have scrolled up, your place stays put and "New lines below" takes
you down. What the app tells your companion to do (pass a reminder on, ask about your bedtime, put right an agenda
change that didn't go through) isn't shown, only their answer; tick "Show what the app tells" them to see those
lines too, each boxed and marked "App to" them (the tick is remembered; lines from before this version always
show). Click one of your companion's replies (it lights up under the mouse) to see what they had in mind for it, and
click it again or press Close to put it away. It comes in two groups: "Kept in mind for every reply" (their most
important facts) and "Recalled for this reply" (what came back with what was just said: smaller facts, what used to be
true, notes from their diary, old talk). Each says what it is in words ("About you", "About you two", "About" them,
"No longer true", their diary, "From an earlier talk") with its date. Forget there and they forget it from then on;
Edit corrects a fact. The talk itself can't be changed: forgetting old talk only stops them recalling it, and the line
stays in the chat. Replies from before this version kept nothing, and don't react to a click. Tray → "Memory book…" shows all of it, a tab each (notes: yours, agenda, facts, diary: their log, memos,
forget): edit, pin (always in mind, upkeep leaves it alone) or delete anything. Each change is saved as you make it,
as in Settings: a field when you leave it (Enter, or a click elsewhere), a tick or a choice at once; a × asks once
more ("Forget?") and forgets on the second click. The line beside Close says what was saved and when, and whether a
change is waiting for you to choose. Only what you changed is written. While it is open it follows what upkeep,
their replies, reminders and the chat history change, except a field you have changed and not yet left; if they
changed that same thing meanwhile, the row shows theirs and asks which to keep. Closing with that unanswered keeps
yours (a fact of theirs written over is kept under "Retired by the review", as changed to yours). A time that can't
be read can't be saved: Close says so first, and a second Close closes without that one change. "Clear the chat" starts the conversation afresh and keeps what they remember;
"Forget all with …" clears the conversation and everything about the two of you; "Forget all about me" clears what
every character knows about you, and your agenda, and leaves the conversations. It stays in the profile folder
(`companion\memory.db`); the key and a ChatGPT sign-in are encrypted for your Windows account. Without a provider they keep to their canned
lines.
